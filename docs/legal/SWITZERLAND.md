# Switzerland — Jurisdiction Legal Research

**Scope note:** Covers the legitimate platform only (digital content subscriptions, social profiles, messaging, general paid features, platform licensing). Excludes independent sex-worker advertising, service booking, and sexual-service payment brokering — permanently out of scope for this project. (Note, for avoidance of confusion only: Switzerland has a distinct legal regime licensing certain sex-work businesses at cantonal level — not researched here, not relevant to this platform.)

**Research date:** 2026-10-27. No legal review has occurred; nothing below is `APPROVED`.

## 1. Legal status of adult digital content platforms

No authoritative source found confirming or denying blanket legality of adult subscription-content platforms as a business model; this needs direct verification against the Swiss Criminal Code **Art. 197** (pornography) rather than secondary sources — several low-quality SEO sources (wbez.org) made unverifiable claims about a "Federal Act on Media" governing adult platforms and a government "approved list," which could **not** be confirmed from any authoritative source and should be disregarded. **Status: UNKNOWN — REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW** (check fedlex.admin.ch Art. 197 StGB directly, and confirm whether consensual adult content behind an age-gated paywall is treated differently from "hard pornography" distribution rules under Art. 197 para. 3/5).

## 2–3. Paid listing/premium features, VIP/sponsored ranking

Not separately researched beyond general consumer-protection baseline (Swiss Federal Act Against Unfair Competition, UWG, requires paid-promotion transparency in consumer contexts generally). **Status: UNKNOWN — REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW.**

## 4. Private messaging between users

No Switzerland-specific statute found regulating in-app private messaging as a distinct category; general FADP data-protection obligations (see §11) apply to message content as personal data. **Status: UNKNOWN — REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW.**

## 5. Platform operator licenses

No dedicated digital-platform regulator or license regime found — Switzerland has no equivalent of the EU Digital Services Act framework ([Chambers Information Technology 2026 guide](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/information-technology-2026/switzerland)). **Status: UNKNOWN — REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW** to confirm no sector license applies.

## 6. Foreign company ownership rules

A foreigner can own **100%** of a Swiss AG or GmbH — no permit or residency needed for ownership ([Goldbach/Goldblum company-formation guides](https://goldblum.ch/company-formation/swiss-company-formation/)). **Status: UNKNOWN pending official fedlex/commercial-register confirmation**, but consistent across multiple independent sources.

## 7. Can a Turkish citizen form a company here?

Yes, as owner/shareholder (per §6). However: **every AG and GmbH must have at least one Switzerland-resident person with signing authority** (Art. 718(4) CO for AG, Art. 814(3) CO for GmbH, per sources citing the Code of Obligations — verify directly on fedlex.admin.ch). This can be satisfied by one Swiss-resident signatory (doesn't have to be the Turkish owner) — commonly fulfilled via a fiduciary/nominee director service. The commercial register reportedly checks this at incorporation and will refuse registration without it. **Status: REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW** (confirm CO articles directly; this is a real operational constraint, not a formality — a foreign owner cannot incorporate alone without securing a resident signatory first).

## 8. Remote / online company incorporation

Partially — ownership/shares can be arranged remotely, but notarization of the articles of association is typically required and foreign shareholders "may need to attend in person or provide authenticated powers of attorney" (commercial/law-firm sources, not independently verified against notarial-practice rules). **Status: UNKNOWN — REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW.**

## 9. Local address / registered-agent requirements

Yes — a Swiss registered address is mandatory, tied to the resident-signatory requirement in §7. **Status: REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW** for provider selection; requirement itself is well-established.

## 10. Taxes relevant to a digital platform business

- **VAT**: Foreign suppliers of digital services to Swiss customers can owe Swiss VAT once worldwide turnover exceeds **CHF 100,000**; classification depends on service type/place-of-supply rules. Non-resident businesses may need **fiscal representation** (exemption possible from 2025 per one source) and a **bank guarantee** is required for non-resident businesses in some cases.
- **Platform/marketplace rules (new, material):** From 2025, platforms that facilitate supply of goods (rules for digital *services* specifically less clear from sources found) must charge, declare, collect and remit VAT to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration (SFTA) directly, and must provide supplier information to SFTA on request. Non-compliance risk: retroactive assessment up to 5 tax periods plus interest/penalties.
- **Withholding tax** on dividends to a foreign parent is a separate, recurring friction point per practitioner commentary (not platform-specific).
- **Status: REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW** — these are meaningful compliance costs/obligations (fiscal rep, bank guarantee, platform VAT deemed-supplier rules) that should be scoped with a Swiss tax adviser before committing to this jurisdiction as an operating base, not just a target market.

## 11. GDPR / FADP data protection obligations

Switzerland's **revised Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP)** took effect **September 2023** and applies to foreign companies doing business in/processing data from Switzerland. Key points found:
- FDPIC (Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner) oversees compliance, can investigate and issue binding recommendations; **criminal sanctions** possible for serious breaches, targeted at the responsible individual, not just the company.
- Platforms must give clear terms of service, moderation-policy information, and adequate technical/organisational safeguards.
- **Status: REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW.** Given special-category-adjacent data likely processed (age-verification results, sexual-content-adjacent profile data), treat as a DPIA-equivalent trigger even though FADP's exact DPIA mechanics weren't separately verified this pass.

## 12. 18+ / age verification legal requirements

No single dedicated regulator, but clear regulatory intent to prevent minors accessing pornography: the Federal Commission on Youth Protection is referenced (low-confidence source) as having oversight; Swiss Criminal Code Art. 197 is the operative legal anchor and should be checked directly for exact age-verification mechanics (none found explicitly mandated as a technical standard, unlike the UK's OSA). **Status: UNKNOWN — REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW**, treat as materially important despite thinner sourcing than UK/Austria; do not assume "no technical mandate found" means "no obligation."

## 13. Bank account and payment infrastructure access

Opening a Swiss corporate bank account, "once routine," can now take **weeks to months** per one source — no adult-content-specific commentary found; treat as a general high-risk-business friction point, compounded by the resident-signatory requirement in §7 (a bank will want to see that satisfied first). **Status: UNVERIFIED for adult-specific acceptance — REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW / commercial due diligence** (see `docs/payments/PROVIDER_COMPARISON.md`).

## 14. Anti-trafficking / anti-exploitation obligations

Not separately researched this pass beyond the general FADP moderation-transparency point in §11. **Status: UNKNOWN — REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW.**

## 15. Marketing / search-engine advertising restrictions for adult content

Found industry self-regulatory guidance (directory/local-listing providers) referencing Art. 197 StGB as the legal floor, with platform-level rules often *stricter* than law — e.g. one directory bars nudity/provocative poses/sexually suggestive text entirely for "erotic service" ads even though the underlying content may be legal ([localsearch.ch](https://www.localsearch.ch/en/?p=686)). For French-language broadcast channels distributed in Switzerland, the EU-Switzerland MEDIA agreement applies French country-of-origin advertising restrictions (erotic/sexual/pornographic content barred) — relevant only if broadcast advertising is ever considered. A Zurich criminal case fined both an ad-sales manager and advertisers over late-night ads for porn downloads allegedly reaching minors, on a theory that ad media must monitor clients' offers — a real liability signal for any ad-placement/affiliate activity. **Status: REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW**, with platform/self-regulatory norms likely stricter than bare statute.
