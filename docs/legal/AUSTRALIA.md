# Jurisdiction: Australia

Scope note: out of scope for this research — independent sex-worker advertising, service booking, sexual-service payment brokering (module D). Everything below concerns only the legitimate platform (digital content subscriptions, social profiles, messaging, general premium features, platform operator status). Accessed date for all items: 2026-10-27.

Research standards: no finding below is a legal opinion. `APPROVED` is never self-assigned by research; it requires actual counsel review, permits, and a recorded operator decision.

## 1. Legal status of adult digital content platforms (OnlyFans-style)
Legal as an ordinary business, but materially constrained by the Online Safety Act 2021 framework (see #12) and state/territory classification law for the content itself. Australia is actively the most aggressive jurisdiction in this research set on age-assurance enforcement for exactly this category — treat this as the single most important Australia-specific finding.
- Status: `REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW`

## 2. Paid listing / premium feature publishing (general)
No sector-specific restriction found beyond Australian Consumer Law (ACL) general conduct rules (misleading/deceptive conduct, unfair contract terms) that apply to any paid digital feature.
- Status: `REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW`

## 3. VIP profile / sponsored ranking features
Same general ACL framework as #2; no sector-specific rule found.
- Status: `UNKNOWN`

## 4. Private messaging between users
Subject to the Online Safety Act 2021's broader "Basic Online Safety Expectations" and the eSafety Commissioner's complaints/takedown powers (cyberbullying, image-based abuse, etc.), which apply to any service with messaging/content-sharing functionality, not just adult content specifically.
- Status: `REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW`

## 5. Platform operator licenses
No general platform-operator license beyond the Online Safety Act's industry-code compliance obligations (#1, #12), which function as a de facto conduct licensing regime for "class 1/2 material" (pornographic/high-impact content) services.
- Status: `REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW`

## 6. Foreign company ownership rules
No general restriction on foreign shareholding of an Australian proprietary (Pty Ltd) company found; the constraint is on **directors**, not shareholders (see #7). Foreign Investment Review Board (FIRB) notification could apply to some transactions but is not expected to be triggered by ordinary company incorporation with no Australian land/sensitive-asset acquisition — confirm with counsel for the specific structure.
- Status: `REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW`
- Source: [Sprintlaw, "Australian Resident Director Requirements: Compliance Guide"](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/australian-resident-director-requirements-compliance-guide/), accessed 2026-10-27

## 7. Can a Turkish citizen form a company there?
Yes as shareholder, with the same structural constraint as Canada/NZ: **an Australian proprietary (Pty Ltd) company must have at least one director who is "ordinarily resident" in Australia** (cited to Corporations Act 2001 s201A in secondary sources; not independently verified against the Act text this pass). A Turkish citizen with no Australian residency cannot be the company's sole director without a resident co-director (a relocating founder, a trusted Australia-based individual, or a professional resident-director service). "Ordinarily resident" is a factual residence test, not citizenship or visa status.
- Status: `REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW`
- Source: [Sprintlaw, "Australian Resident Director Requirements"](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/australian-resident-director-requirements-compliance-guide/); [Lawpath, "How Overseas Founders Can Incorporate a Company in Australia"](https://lawpath.com.au/blog/overseas-founders-incorporate-in-australia/3) — accessed 2026-10-27

## 8. Remote / online company incorporation
ASIC company registration can be completed remotely by a foreign founder online, subject to the resident-director requirement (#7) and standard identity-verification (director ID number — "Director ID" — is mandatory for all company directors, including foreign nationals, obtained via the Australian Business Registry Services).
- Status: `REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW`

## 9. Local address / registered-agent requirements
An Australian Pty Ltd company must maintain a registered office address in Australia (can be the resident director's address or a professional registered-office service) and a principal place of business address.
- Status: `REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW`

## 10. Taxes relevant to a digital platform business
Standard Australian company income tax (currently 25% for base-rate entities / 30% standard rate, not independently re-verified this pass) plus GST (10%) — Australia's GST rules extend to "inbound intangible consumer supplies" from non-resident suppliers of digital products/services to Australian consumers, a separate registration/compliance question from where the company itself is incorporated.
- Status: `REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW`

## 11. Data protection obligations
The **Privacy Act 1988** (Cth), enforced by the OAIC, governs personal information handling via the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs). A material and non-obvious finding: Australia has a **small business exemption** — organizations with annual turnover of AUD 3 million or less are generally exempt from the Act, UNLESS they fall into carved-out categories (health service providers holding health information, traders in personal information, AML/CTF "reporting entities," Commonwealth contractors, or those with an "Australian link" that opt in). A platform handling sensitive/health-adjacent data or operating at scale should not assume the small-business exemption applies without specific review — and one source (unverified independently this pass) indicates the exemption itself may be under legislative review/possible removal.
- Status: `REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW`
- Source: multiple secondary summaries of Privacy Act 1988 s6D/s5B/s6EA, e.g. [HWL Ebsworth](https://hwlebsworth.com.au/privacy-for-small-businesses/) — accessed 2026-10-27; confirm current exemption status directly with OAIC before relying on it.

## 12. 18+ / age verification legal requirements — material finding
Australia is actively enforcing two parallel, overlapping age-assurance regimes directly relevant to this project:
- **Online porn age-assurance industry codes**, registered by the eSafety Commissioner, require services that host online pornography to apply "appropriate age assurance measures," with the main obligations taking effect **9 March 2026**. Non-compliance exposes services to potential civil penalties described as in the millions of AUD. The codes also restrict use of personal information collected for age assurance in ways that would breach privacy law.
- **Social Media Minimum Age (SMMA)** law: since 10 December 2025, specified social media platforms must take "reasonable steps" to prevent under-16s from creating/holding accounts; self-declaration alone is explicitly insufficient. Penalties up to **AUD 49.5 million** for non-compliant platforms. The eSafety Commissioner began an evaluation of the SMMA regime around 26 February 2026 (status: under deliberation per one tracker).
- A social-content platform with a subscription/adult-content component plausibly sits at the intersection of both regimes depending on its content mix and whether it is captured as a "social media platform" under the SMMA definition — this needs explicit legal scoping, not assumption either way.
- Status: `REQUIRES_LEGAL_REVIEW` — highest-priority item for this jurisdiction.
- Sources: [eSafety Commissioner / Baker McKenzie, "Australia Phase 2 Online Safety Codes Registered"](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/alerts/2025/09/australia-phase-2-online-safety-codes-registered-by-esafety-commissioner); [Department of Infrastructure, "Social media minimum age"](https://infrastructure.gov.au/media-communications/internet/online-safety/current-legislation/social-media-minimum-age); [AVPA, "Australia's World-Leading Age Verification Laws"](https://avpassociation.com/australias-world-leading-age-verification-laws/) — accessed 2026-10-27
